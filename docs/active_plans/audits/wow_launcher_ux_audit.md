# Launcher curriculum UX audit

Date: 2026-07-22

## Decision

Adopt direction A: a two-mode "Choose your lab experience" launcher. Put a small,
curated row of full experimental pathways first, then reveal focused practice by
curriculum cluster. Keep every card a normal anchor and use only generated metadata
already available to the launcher. This is the clearest improvement with the least
risk: it makes the existing `sequence_runner` versus `mini_protocol` distinction
student-facing without adding YAML fields or coupling the launcher to scene runtime.

Do not begin with a curriculum map or time promises. The current index does not expose
safe duration data, prerequisite order, or a reliable one-to-one lesson hierarchy.

## Evidence and current state

Screenshot reviewed: `test-results/smoke_00_launcher.png`, generated by
`tests/playwright/smoke.spec.ts:48` at 1280 x 720.

Observed above the fold:

- The page presents "Full protocols" before "Mini-protocols", but does not explain
  the student choice or the different intended use of each tier.
- All five visible full-protocol cards have the same visual weight. A student has no
  clear first choice or primary action.
- The durable student-facing title is followed by a machine-facing snake_case ID.
  This consumes visual attention but does not help a student select a learning task.
- The card description begins with the mandated authoring phrase "Overall, this
  protocol aims to accomplish..." and is cut after about two lines. The useful
  scientific activity is usually beyond the visible fragment.
- Step count appears as a small badge. It expresses workload but not what the student
  will learn, whether the choice is a complete experiment or practice, or an expected
  time commitment.
- "Mini-protocols" begins below the 720 px viewport, so the launcher's first impression
  makes the large pathways look like the only choices.

The screenshot proves the visible structure and above-fold composition only. It does
not prove mobile behavior, hover behavior, keyboard behavior, or whether every card
is visually consistent below the fold.

## Design directions

### A. Choose your lab experience (recommended)

Make the first screen teach the difference between the two existing protocol kinds.
The top section becomes "Run a complete experiment" and includes the three complete,
end-to-end runners first. The lower section becomes "Practice a focused technique",
with cluster headings such as "Cell culture" and "SDS-PAGE". Batch runners remain
in a separate "Repeat a technique" group or inside the full-experiment collection;
they should not compete with end-to-end pathways as a student's first choice.

Each card gets a short kind label, human title, one action-oriented learning hook,
step-count burden, and an explicit primary action such as "Start full experiment" or
"Practice this technique". The whole card remains the anchor; the CTA is an in-card
visual affordance, not a nested button.

Why it fits now:

- It uses `protocol_type`, `display_title`, `cluster`, `learning_goal_hook`, and
  `step_count`, all present in the slim index.
- It reinforces the contract's distinction that large protocols are sequences of
  focused mini-protocols (`docs/PRIMARY_CONTRACT.md:16-17`).
- It is a presentation and codegen-copy change, not a new authoring vocabulary.

Risk: a priority order among full experiments is an instructional choice. Start with
neutral visual ordering unless the instructor explicitly identifies a course sequence.

### B. Browse by laboratory story

Replace protocol-type tiers with two big curriculum stories: "Measure cell viability"
and "Separate proteins by size". Cards appear as chapters in each story, with a
start-here card and expandable technique practice.

Why it could be compelling: it gives non-expert students a scientific reason for each
action before they see a list of instrument tasks.

Why not first: current cluster names are implementation-oriented, and the slim index
does not encode authoritative story names, prerequisite order, or membership. A hard-
coded UI map would become a second curriculum authority and drift from YAML.

### C. Interactive curriculum map

Offer a visual flow diagram: question -> technique -> result, with full protocols as
paths and mini-protocols as nodes. Selecting a node reveals the purpose, workload, and
start action.

Why it could be the highest visual impact: it matches the repository's flow-first
pedagogy and can make a long protocol feel navigable.

Why defer: runner composition is not exposed in the slim index, individual mini-
protocols can recur in a runner, and no learner progress exists. A diagram would
either hide complexity inaccurately or require a new derived curriculum view plus
careful instructional review.

## Recommended information architecture

The intended student path is:

```text
Landing
  |
  +-- Run a complete experiment
  |     |-- Cell viability dose-response       [Start full experiment]
  |     |-- SDS-PAGE: protein separation       [Start full experiment]
  |     +-- Routine cell passage                [Start full experiment]
  |
  +-- Practice a focused technique
        |-- Cell culture                         [Browse 10 techniques]
        +-- SDS-PAGE                             [Browse 16 techniques]
              |
              +-- Technique card                 [Practice this technique]
```

Counts in this wireframe are illustrative of the current index snapshot, not canonical
curriculum claims. The implementation should derive any visible collection count from
the index at runtime.

Suggested above-fold wireframe:

```text
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| VIRTUAL LABS                                                              |
| Learn a complete experiment, or practice one lab technique at a time.    |
|                                                                          |
| Choose your lab experience                                                |
|                                                                          |
| [ FULL EXPERIMENT ]      [ FULL EXPERIMENT ]      [ FULL EXPERIMENT ]    |
| Cell viability assay      SDS-PAGE separation      Routine cell passage  |
| From prepared cells to    Prepare, run, stain,     Detach, pellet, and   |
| an absorbance readout.    and image a protein gel. reseed cells.         |
| 64 guided steps           72 guided steps           18 guided steps       |
| Start full experiment ->  Start full experiment ->  Start full experiment|
|                                                                          |
| Need one skill? Practice a focused technique.                            |
| [ Cell culture (10) ]                 [ SDS-PAGE (16) ]                  |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

The display copy above is a design target, not approved canonical curriculum text.
Before implementation, derive it from existing `learning.goals` or `learning.outcomes`
and have a biology instructor review every high-level path title and hook.

## Copy and interaction rules

- Hide `protocol_name` in the normal student view. Retain it only as
  `data-protocol-id` for stable navigation and tests. If debugging exposure is needed,
  place it behind an instructor/developer-only surface rather than every learner card.
- Replace the clipped boilerplate hook with a short sentence that begins with the lab
  action or result. Example transformation: "Overall, this mini-protocol aims to
  accomplish preparing..." becomes "Prepare 1 L of working SDS-PAGE running buffer."
  The source remains `learning`; this does not add an author field.
- Label kind plainly: "Full experiment" for `sequence_runner` and "Focused practice"
  for `mini_protocol`. Do not expose internal terms such as "sequence runner".
- Use "guided steps" rather than an unexplained "steps" badge. Do not equate steps
  with minutes or difficulty.
- Use a visible text CTA on every card. A card remains one anchor so normal click,
  middle-click, and open-in-new-tab behavior remains intact.
- Collapse each practice cluster initially after its first compact row, with a visible
  "Show all N techniques" control. This is progressive disclosure, not filtering out
  curriculum content.
- Do not use a generic dashboard, progress bar, points, badges, or "resume" language
  until the runtime has a truthful persisted-progress model.

## Acceptance criteria

- The first viewport states in plain language that students may run a complete
  experiment or practice a focused technique.
- Each visible pathway card exposes one student-readable title, one action/result
  learning hook, a kind label, a guided-step count, and a visible primary action.
- No normal student card visibly shows a snake_case `protocol_name`.
- A full-experiment card and a focused-practice card are distinguishable without
  relying on color alone.
- Every card is a single native anchor to `<protocol_name>.html`; middle-click and
  open-in-new-tab continue to work.
- Each practice collection can be expanded and collapsed through a visible control;
  the control announces the number of hidden or shown items in its text.
- The launcher renders every index entry exactly once, preserving the current
  `data-protocol-id` contract used by `tests/playwright/test_launcher.spec.ts:84-107`.
- The smoke test still reaches a mini-protocol through a visible card click and saves
  an updated launcher screenshot, consistent with `docs/PRIMARY_CONTRACT.md:26-31`.
- Desktop screenshots at 1280 x 720 and a narrow mobile viewport show the top choice
  and a path to focused practice without clipping text or making the CTA ambiguous.
- The build and TypeScript checks pass; no generated file is hand-edited.

## Constraints and residual risk

- No YAML vocabulary changes are needed or proposed. The authoring surface remains
  closed, as required by `docs/PRIMARY_DESIGN.md:82-105`.
- Any new derived launcher field belongs in `pipeline/gen_protocols.py`, its TypeScript
  index type, and regenerated output. It must not be a hand-maintained map in the UI.
- The current title derivation is mechanically humanized, not editorially curated.
  Several titles remain awkward (for example, "Plate drug treatment drug addition").
  A generic formatter cannot reliably solve that without either a reviewed derived
  transform or an approved canonical authoring field.
- The current hook is intentionally truncated to 80 characters. This is the immediate
  source of the unhelpful ellipses and should be replaced by a generated, sentence-
  shaped derivation with a clear test suite.
- Duration must remain absent until a reliable generator or walkthrough measurement
  defines what the estimate means and covers all protocols.
- A full curriculum sequence must remain deferred until ordering and runner-membership
  semantics are intentionally exposed and validated. Alphabetical order is not a
  pedagogical sequence.

## Validation plan

1. Update the launcher component and styles without importing runtime or scene code.
2. If changing generated copy, update `pipeline/gen_protocols.py`, the slim type, and
   generated output through the normal pipeline.
3. Extend the existing launcher Playwright test to assert student-facing kind labels,
   CTA text, no visible protocol ID, expand/collapse behavior, and intact hrefs.
4. Run the launcher smoke path and inspect fresh 1280 x 720 plus narrow-viewport
   screenshots. Verify a real visible card click still opens a real scene.
5. Have a biology instructor review every top-level pathway title and learning hook
   for scientific and pedagogical accuracy before calling the copy complete.
